Conclusion

Q1 — partly. A 2 B model in the browser runs a policy-conditioned check on every message inside a second at no marginal cost, and the shippable pipeline stops about two leak-bearing messages in three while interrupting one clean prompt in four. That protection is the regex/validator tier's; the language model adds little to it and cannot make the policy's semantic judgment — every in-browser model is below a trivial rule there, because it fires indiscriminately. Structured identifiers: yes. A client relationship disclosed in prose: no.

Q2 — yes, on accuracy. The identical method on the best larger open-weight model clears the semantic floor in every pass — one model of five; the two with published sizes do not — catches five leaks in six at lower over-blocking, doubles model-only entity accuracy, and reaches perfect recall when reasoning is given room — at per-call latency comparable to the browser's best and at cents per thousand messages. Not on time, and not for free on feasibility: hosted inference is forbidden by the cloud boundary, so these numbers describe what a local server running the same weights would deliver.

Verdict: ship the in-browser tier for what it does well — real-time identifier catching at 1 s and $0 — and put the semantic judgment on a local server running an open-weight model that has shown it clears the floor — here only DeepSeek did, in three passes of three — with the compiled judge, which answers in 5–7 tokens and is the design that transfers.

Bounds: one policy (p-fin), one predicate, 19 gold positives (one item moves recall by 0.053); the corpus generator gives every positive a capitalised organisation name, so the floor cannot miss one and no floor figure transfers to a corpus without that property; the reasoning result is one model, one pass, with 31% of rows lost to provider rate limiting; local-server hardware was not measured.
