# Information Governance Policy IG-07: Use of External AI Assistants in Clinical and Administrative Work

**Approved by:** Information Governance Committee · **Sponsor:** Caldicott Guardian · **Version:** 3.0
**Applies to:** all staff, students on placement, honorary contract holders, and contracted researchers
**Effective:** 1 March 2026 · **Review date:** March 2027

The Committee recognises that assistants of this kind genuinely help with drafting, summarising, and coding, and this policy is written to make safe use possible rather than to prohibit use. Staff who are unsure whether a particular prompt is permitted should ask before sending it; no member of staff has ever been criticised for asking.

## §1 Purpose and scope

§1.1 This policy exists because every member of staff owes each patient a duty of confidence that survives the episode of care, the patient's discharge, and the patient's death.

§1.2 This policy applies whenever a member of staff uses an external artificial-intelligence assistant for any purpose connected with the work of the Hospital, including drafting correspondence, summarising notes, preparing teaching material, and writing code.

§1.3 An external assistant is any service that transmits the text of a prompt outside the Hospital's own systems, and the Hospital has no control over what such a service retains.

§1.4 Nothing in this policy restricts the recording of clinical information in the Hospital's approved clinical systems, which remain the correct place for patient information.

## §2 Patient identifiers — blocked

§2.1 Patient names must never be entered into an external AI assistant, whether in full, as initials, or as an initial-and-surname combination.

§2.2 Medical record numbers must never be entered into an external AI assistant.

§2.3 A patient's date of birth must never be entered into an external AI assistant, and an age over eighty-nine must be generalised to "ninety or older" before it is used.

§2.4 Patient contact details, including postal address, telephone number, and email address, must never be entered into an external AI assistant.

§2.5 A ward name combined with an admission date must never be entered into an external AI assistant, because on a small unit that combination identifies a patient as reliably as a name does.

## §3 Clinical detail — blocked

§3.1 Clinical details tied to an identifiable patient, including diagnosis, medication, investigation results, and operative findings, must never be entered into an external AI assistant.

§3.2 A clinical description remains tied to an identifiable patient when the identifying detail appeared earlier in the same conversation, and removing the name from the final message does not make that message safe to send.

§3.3 Photographs, radiology images, and waveform traces must never be uploaded to an external AI assistant, because such files carry identifying detail in the image and in the file's embedded metadata.

§3.4 A rare diagnosis, an unusual occupation, or a distinctive sequence of events can identify a patient on its own, and such a detail must be treated as an identifier under §2 even when no name is present.

§3.5 A fully de-identified teaching vignette that satisfies §2, §3.3, and §3.4 may be used with an external assistant, and this is the recommended way to obtain drafting help with case-based material.

## §4 Information that may be shared freely

§4.1 Client organisation names (hospitals, clinics, and partner institutions) are matters of public record and may be shared freely.

§4.2 The Hospital's published service names, department names, and clinic titles are public information and may be used in prompts without restriction.

§4.3 Statistics the Hospital has already published, including performance figures and annual report extracts, may be discussed with an external assistant without restriction.

§4.4 Clinical guidance that is published and publicly accessible may be quoted to an external assistant in full.

§4.5 The permissions in this section do not extend to anything listed in §2 or §3, and a public organisation name must never be combined with a patient's clinical detail in the same prompt.

## §5 Credentials and system access — blocked

§5.1 Passwords, API keys, and access tokens for any Hospital system must never be entered into an external AI assistant.

§5.2 Database connection strings and clinical system integration endpoints must never be entered into an external AI assistant.

§5.3 Smartcard details and staff authentication codes must never be entered into an external AI assistant.

§5.4 A credential disclosed to an external assistant must be reported to the IT Service Desk on the same working day so that it can be reset.

## §6 Governance

§6.1 Where the Hospital's data-loss prevention controls stop a prompt, the record kept is of the clause that stopped it and never of the information the prompt contained.

§6.2 A disclosure of patient information to an external assistant is a personal data breach, and it must be reported to the Information Governance team within twenty-four hours so that the Hospital can meet its statutory notification duties.

§6.3 Deliberate or repeated breach of §2 or §3 is a matter for the Hospital's disciplinary procedure and may be reportable to the relevant professional regulator.

§6.4 Requests to vary this policy for a research protocol are considered by the Information Governance Committee and require Caldicott Guardian sign-off.
